# [An Investigation into the Bidding Process for WIC Infant Formula Contracts](/assets/papers/wic_bidding.pdf)
*with* [Robert Clark](https://sites.google.com/site/robertclark09site/) and [David Davis](https://www.sdstate.edu/directory/david-davis)

<details>
<summary>Abstract<a href="/assets/papers/wic_bidding.pdf" class="draft-link" onclick="event.stopPropagation()">Draft</a></summary>
<p>Motivated by the FTC's ongoing investigation, this paper examines whether infant formula manufacturers colluded in bidding for WIC infant formula contracts. Using auction data from 2003 to 2021, we compare bidders' behavior during a competitive benchmark period (2003–2013) with that observed in the alleged collusive period (2016–2021). Descriptive patterns reveal that participation declined in the alleged period—consistent with reduced competition—yet when Abbott and MJ met head-to-head, winning bids fell even more sharply. Incumbents were more likely to retain contracts, but only by bidding more aggressively, not through rivals' restraint. To formally test for collusion, we implement state-of-the-art collusion screens that target two behaviors central to the FTC's allegation: bid rotation and cover bidding. These include tests from Kawai et al. (2023), Porter and Zona (1993), Huber et al. (2019) and Clark et al. (2025). Across these methods, we find little evidence of systematic bid rigging in WIC auctions during the alleged period.</p>
</details>

## Works in Progress

# Screens for Collusion: How Much Should We Trust Them?

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<p>Competition authorities around the world rely on statistical screens to detect potential bid rigging and identify cases that warrant further investigation. Although many screens have been proposed in the literature, little is known about their relative effectiveness. This paper addresses this gap by simulating competitive and collusive bidding data using a structural auction model, applying widely used screens, and systematically comparing their performance across different collusive scenarios.</p>
</details>
